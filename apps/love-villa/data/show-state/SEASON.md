# Season 1 — Love Villa: Nations

**Theme.** Nine nations, one villa, and not a single secret that survives the week — narrated by The Rafter Voice, an original off-screen gossip who knows everything and forgives nothing.

Ten episodes, one villa, nine adults from nine countries who all think they're the sensible one. The season opens on a two-way crush collision over Lucas and escalates every episode as the villa's buried secrets surface one by one: Lucas can't swim, Rohan's famous compatibility algorithm is astrology in a spreadsheet, Poppy keeps a notebook of catastrophically sincere poetry, Matteo's mozzarella devotion is a lactose-intolerant lie, Sienna carries a 48-hour engagement ring on a keyring, Brock's seven-figure startup has forty-three dollars in it, Élodie sobs at rom-com trailers, Greta cries at dog videos at 22:15 sharp, and Alejandro's smolder took eleven months of mirror practice. Every reveal reroutes a couple. Every nation is equally ridiculous and equally loved. By the finale, deflection loses, sincerity wins, and Poppy reads the poem out loud on purpose.

Review this file for the "season" approval checkpoint.

## Episode 1: Sleep-Talking Sabotage

- **Hook:** Poppy talks in her sleep. Élodie is wide awake.
- **Twist:** Élodie reveals she already had a two-minute kiss with Lucas on day two — before Poppy's feelings even had a name. Poppy laughs. The laugh does not reach her eyes.
- **Engagement:** Alejandro says he'll take one person off the board by sunrise. Who does he name? Guess before Episode 2.
- **Focus:** poppy-uk, elodie-france, lucas-brazil, brock-usa
- **Connects:** Season opener — plants the Poppy/Élodie/Lucas triangle that reroutes every couple through Episode 10, and delivers Alejandro as the season's chaos engine.

Day six. The villa's four starter pairs look stable — Brock with Élodie, Matteo with Poppy, Lucas with Sienna, Rohan with Greta — and every single one is a clerical error. At 3am Poppy murmurs a name that is absolutely not Matteo's. Élodie hears it, says nothing, and rates the moment a six. By breakfast the narrator has confirmed the horror: Poppy and Élodie want the same Brazilian. Poppy attacks the feeling with jokes. Élodie attacks it with indifference so convincing she nearly deletes herself from the running. Lucas, oblivious, hugs both of them within ninety seconds and starts a fire he cannot name. Brock mistakes Élodie's politeness for a proposal and begins planning a joint venture. Matteo senses a disturbance in the loyalty field and preemptively makes lasagne for eleven. Then the gate opens.

## Episode 2: The Spaniard Arrives Late, On Purpose

- **Hook:** Alejandro says a name at sunrise. Greta drops her clipboard.
- **Twist:** Alejandro admits — to Brock, of all people — that he chose Élodie because she's the only one who didn't look at him. Brock immediately tells four people it's 'a strategy pivot'.
- **Engagement:** Rohan kept Greta's secret. Would you have? Tell us in the comments.
- **Focus:** alejandro-spain, elodie-france, greta-germany, rohan-india, matteo-italy
- **Connects:** Direct payoff of Episode 1's cliffhanger; Greta's 22:15 secret revealed here becomes the emotional proof of Rohan's loyalty in Episodes 4 and 9.

Alejandro picks Élodie, and Élodie — furious at her own crush — says yes purely to prove she's unbothered. Poppy celebrates for four seconds before realising the field being clear means she now has to actually say something. She says a joke instead. Matteo, freed and devastated, begins an operatic three-course grieving process and accidentally cooks the best meal of the season. Brock, unpaired and unbothered, appoints himself Alejandro's biggest fan, which ruins Alejandro's mystique entirely. Meanwhile Rohan follows a noise at 22:15 and finds Greta locked in the bathroom with the fan on, weeping at a video of a dog reuniting with a soldier. He does not tease her. He brings tissues, sits outside the door, and mentions the exact brand of tea she said she liked on day one. Greta reschedules her entire evening block.

## Episode 3: The Deep End

- **Hook:** Sienna dares Lucas into the deep end. Lucas cannot swim.
- **Twist:** Lucas doesn't thank Poppy for saving him. He thanks her for not laughing. That single sentence moves the whole season.
- **Engagement:** Poppy nearly said the real thing at the pool steps. Tomorrow, someone finds her notebook.
- **Focus:** lucas-brazil, poppy-uk, sienna-australia, matteo-italy
- **Connects:** Pays off Episode 1's 'why won't Lucas pass the pool steps'; Sienna's deflection here sets up her ring reveal in Episode 7, and Poppy's dropped bag sets up Episode 5.

Challenge day: a raft race across the pool with couples' loyalty riding on it. Sienna, allergic to sincerity in its final second, converts a genuinely tender conversation with Lucas into a dare — and dares him off the deep end. Lucas, six days into faking it, goes in grinning and does not come back up smiling. Poppy is in the water before the narrator finishes the sentence. Union Jack crop top, full sprint, no jokes. She hauls him to the steps and the villa goes silent for the first time all season. Lucas confesses in front of everyone: beach volleyball champion, cannot swim, has been terrified since Tuesday. Sienna's confidence-dealer act collapses; she apologises with her whole chest and then makes a joke to escape it, and Lucas — highest emotional intelligence in the building — clocks the pattern out loud. Matteo declares Poppy a national hero of two nations. Brock offers to teach Lucas 'aquatic synergy'.

## Episode 4: Stars in a Spreadsheet

- **Hook:** Rohan's algorithm is public. Rohan's algorithm is astrology.
- **Twist:** Greta returns with a printed spreadsheet of her own: twelve tabs, one metric, 'Rohan'. He scored 96% too. She'd run the numbers on day two and never mentioned it.
- **Engagement:** Vote: is Rohan's algorithm romantic genius or a horoscope with a lanyard?
- **Focus:** rohan-india, greta-germany, matteo-italy, elodie-france
- **Connects:** Builds on Episode 2's bathroom-door tenderness between Rohan and Greta; the 96% score here is the seed of the Matteo–Élodie romance that lands in Episode 6 and Episode 9.

Villa Compatibility Night: Rohan finally unveils the famous framework that got him on this show. Eleven metrics, three tabs, one lanyard. Poppy asks one question — 'what's column F?' — and column F is 'Moon Sign'. The framework is horoscopes in a spreadsheet wearing business clothes. The villa dies laughing. Brock calls it 'disruptive'. Élodie calls it 'a four, generously'. But the algorithm's top match is Matteo and Élodie at 96%, and both of them go very quiet, which is much worse than laughing. Alejandro, still technically with Élodie, says nothing with tremendous style. Then Greta finds her own score with Rohan: 71%. She reads it three times. She files it. She schedules a walk alone. And Rohan — who remembers every detail anyone has ever mentioned — realises he built a machine that rated the one person he actually likes and forgot to include the data that matters.

## Episode 5: Nine Layers, One Notebook

- **Hook:** Alejandro is holding Poppy's notebook. Out loud. On stage.
- **Twist:** Poppy reads the rest to Lucas that night — and the last poem in the notebook isn't about Lucas at all. It's about her dad, and why she jokes. Lucas doesn't say a word. He just holds the mug.
- **Engagement:** Poppy asked Lucas one question at 2am. His answer is Episode 6's first thirty seconds.
- **Focus:** poppy-uk, alejandro-spain, lucas-brazil, elodie-france
- **Connects:** Direct payoff of the bag Poppy dropped poolside in Episode 3 and the sleep-talking of Episode 1; Élodie's defence here explains her Episode 6 pivot toward Matteo.

Talent Night in the villa courtyard. Alejandro, needing a moment after being emotionally outperformed by a spreadsheet, produces a small battered notebook found by the pool since Episode 3 and reads a poem 'by an anonymous romantic'. It rhymes 'Lucas' with 'ruckus'. Twice. The villa realises whose it is in real time and the temperature drops forty degrees. Poppy does not run. Poppy stands up, takes the notebook back, and says the funniest thing she has ever said, and everyone can hear that her voice is shaking. Lucas is not laughing. Lucas looks like someone just handed him something he'd been waiting for. Élodie — and this is the season's turn — steps in front of Poppy and dismantles Alejandro in four sentences of surgical French honesty, defending the rival she's been quietly resenting for four episodes. Brock hugs everyone, including Alejandro, who deserves nothing.

## Episode 6: The Mozzarella Martyr

- **Hook:** Matteo just won the cheese challenge. Matteo is lactose intolerant.
- **Twist:** Matteo says he'd suffer the cheese again — because his grandmother taught him the recipes and he can't bear to stop cooking them. The joke becomes the most moving speech of the season, and it's eleven seconds long.
- **Engagement:** Élodie said a number out loud. Is a ten from Élodie worth more than a poem? You decide.
- **Focus:** matteo-italy, elodie-france, alejandro-spain, greta-germany
- **Connects:** Cashes in the 96% match from Episode 4 and Élodie's protective streak from Episode 5; Poppy's finance question here directly triggers Episode 8.

Cook-Off Day: the villa is split into two kitchens and Matteo, national representative of dairy, is unbeatable — until Greta, who reads labels, notices he has never once eaten his own creations. Sienna dares him to take one bite of his prize-winning mozzarella tower. He takes three, for honour. The next twenty minutes are a beautiful, agonising lie collapsing in real time. Matteo confesses to the whole villa that he has been suffering nightly for the mozzarella brand, and it is the most Italian sentence ever recorded. Élodie doesn't laugh. Élodie brings him water, sits with him for two hours, tells nobody, and rates him — out loud, for the first time in her life — a ten. Alejandro sees it and, in his one genuinely graceful act of the season, ends things with Élodie before she has to. Brock announces a business idea for lactose-free mozzarella and calls it 'a seven-figure play'. Poppy asks how many figures are currently in his account. Brock changes the subject with astonishing speed.

## Episode 7: 48 Hours in Queensland

- **Hook:** Sienna's keyring has a ring on it. Lucas just noticed.
- **Twist:** Poppy doesn't retaliate. She gives Sienna the notebook to read — the part about her dad — because loyalty is the only thing Poppy has never joked about. Sienna is destroyed by kindness.
- **Engagement:** Two people are now protecting Sienna. One of them has forty-three dollars. Episode 8 opens with a receipt.
- **Focus:** sienna-australia, lucas-brazil, poppy-uk, brock-usa
- **Connects:** Direct payoff of Sienna's Episode 3 deflection and Episode 6 dare; Poppy's page-nine poem from Episode 5 becomes the peace offering, and Brock/Sienna here sets up the finale.

Beach-day storage clear-out. Lucas picks up Sienna's keys and finds an engagement ring threaded on the loop. The villa assumes a prank. Sienna's face says otherwise, and Sienna's face has never said otherwise about anything. Cornered kindly, she admits it: engaged once, for forty-eight hours, to a man she met at a jet-ski rental, and she has carried the ring ever since — not for him, but as proof she once said yes to something without turning it into a dare. Poppy sits down beside her without a single joke, which costs her physically. Then the betrayal: Sienna, terrified of being seen, tells Lucas she likes him — the very man Poppy has been sincerely, publicly, poetically in love with since Episode 1. Lucas says the kindest no in television history and it still lands like a wall. Poppy hears the end of it from the doorway. She says nothing. She says nothing for a very long time.

## Episode 8: Forty-Three Dollars

- **Hook:** Brock's startup pitch is live. Poppy has the bank app.
- **Twist:** Brock's one investor, his mum, sent a message read aloud on the villa speaker — and she doesn't mention the money at all. She asks whether he's remembered to be kind. Matteo has to leave the room.
- **Engagement:** Vote for the villa's most genuinely decent human: Brock, Poppy, or Élodie. Results in Episode 10.
- **Focus:** brock-usa, poppy-uk, sienna-australia, elodie-france
- **Connects:** Pays off the receipt from Episode 7 and Poppy's suspicion from Episode 6; Poppy's public loyalty here proves the character arc set up by her Episode 5 notebook.

Investor Night: the villa pitches passion projects for a prize date. Brock delivers eleven minutes of unstoppable American belief about his seven-figure lemonade-technology company — and Poppy, holding the receipt he dropped in Episode 7, asks the number out loud. Forty-three dollars. One investor. His mum. The villa's reaction is a face-reaction masterpiece: Élodie's eyebrow, Greta's clipboard hitting the tile, Matteo already crying. Brock does the bravest thing anyone does all season: he doesn't backpedal. He says he's broke, he says he hypes everyone else because he needs to believe it works, and he keeps smiling while his voice goes. Then Poppy — the villa's sniper — steps in front of him and defends him harder than she's defended anyone, because that's who she actually is under the sarcasm. And Sienna, unprompted, takes the mic and dares the whole villa to name one time Brock made them feel small. Nobody can. Nine nations, zero examples.

## Episode 9: Four Applications and a Mirror

- **Hook:** Élodie is sobbing at a movie trailer. The whole villa is watching.
- **Twist:** Alejandro withdraws from the final himself — he says he came here to be looked at, and he'd rather leave having actually seen other people. The villa's most arrogant man exits as its most self-aware.
- **Engagement:** Next: the finale. One vote, one poem read on purpose, and one man who cannot swim walking towards the deep end.
- **Focus:** elodie-france, matteo-italy, alejandro-spain, rohan-india, greta-germany
- **Connects:** Cashes the 96% score from Episode 4 and Élodie's Episode 6 'ten'; resolves Alejandro's Episode 5 betrayal and Rohan's stalled confession from Episodes 2 and 4.

Final-week movie night. Somebody puts on a cheesy American rom-com and Élodie — arbiter of the ten-point scale, woman of ice — is gone by the trailer. Openly. Wetly. She owns every one of these films and can quote them. The villa's reaction beat is legendary. Matteo, weeping harder than she is, holds her hand through the entire third act and asks her to be his properly, out loud, with a menu. Élodie, who has spent nine episodes arguing herself out of what she wants, says yes in French, then repeats it in English so there's no confusion. Meanwhile Alejandro finally cracks. He admits he applied to this show four times, that the smolder took eleven months of mirror practice and one pulled facial muscle, and that he read Poppy's poem because he'd never had anything that sincere to read of his own. He apologises to Poppy directly. Poppy accepts, brutally and completely. Rohan, out of drafts, throws away his notebook and just says the sentence to Greta — no plan, no framework, no columns. Greta checks her watch, says he is four days late, and kisses him anyway.

## Episode 10: Full Send

- **Hook:** Lucas is walking to the deep end. Nobody dared him this time.
- **Twist:** Poppy and Lucas split the prize nine ways — including with Alejandro, who already left — and Brock, still forty-three dollars deep, quietly declines his share and asks that it go to Matteo's grandmother's recipe book instead. Élodie cries. On camera. Again.
- **Engagement:** Nine nations, one villa, every secret out. Which pair should we bring back next season? Vote now.
- **Focus:** poppy-uk, lucas-brazil, brock-usa, sienna-australia, matteo-italy, elodie-france
- **Connects:** Closes every thread planted since Episode 1: the sleep-talked name, the poolside notebook, Lucas's non-swimming, Sienna's ring, Brock's forty-three dollars, Matteo's dairy lie, Élodie's tears — and Greta's scheduled reunion sets up Season 2.

Finale. Four pairs, one public vote, and a villa where every secret has already been detonated. Poppy stands up and reads the Lucas poem aloud, on purpose, to his face, in front of everyone, and does not make a single joke — the whole season's arc collapsed into ninety seconds of sincerity that the narrator refuses to interrupt. Lucas answers by walking, unforced and terrified, into the deep end while Poppy holds his hand from the edge; he goes under, comes up, and laughs like a man who's finally stopped performing. Sienna and Brock stop pretending: two loud optimists, one keyring ring finally taken off it, and a hype-man who's never once made her feel small. Matteo cooks the final dinner entirely dairy-free and Élodie rates it a ten in front of nine nations. Rohan and Greta arrive at the ceremony four minutes early because of course they do. The decency vote lands, the winning pair is announced, and the prize gets split in a way nobody expects.
